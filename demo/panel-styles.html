Le tout assemblé — panneaux, tuiles et boutons sous un seul sceau

The radius question is settled and shipped: panels 8px, tiles 4px, buttons 4px. What is not settled is whether one motion language reads as coherent when every component on a page wears it at once — which is a question you can only answer by looking at a whole page, not a gallery of cards. Every padding, gap, background and shadow below is taken from mv-home.css and mv-tiles.css unchanged.

La décision que ce fichier prend à votre place

Sceau frappé, as drawn in the gallery, is a cream button with warm-brown text. Taken literally, putting it on every button repaints the site cream — a palette decision, not a motion one, and a far larger thing than it sounds. So the seal is rebuilt here palette-independently: every component keeps the colours it has today and takes the seal's relief and its recoil. That is the same contract .mv-stamp already made — motion only, no colours — and the reason it survived a dozen foreground/background combinations. The literal reading is at the bottom of this page, so the two can be compared rather than argued about.

One value cannot be derived and so is named twice: --seal-glint. Whether a raised letter catches light or casts shadow depends on whether it is lighter or darker than the surface under it, and CSS has no way to ask — color-mix cannot branch, and light-dark() answers a different question. Dark for light-on-dark, white for dark-on-light. It is the only place a colour is named.

À vérifier avant de déployer

1. The rim is new. Sceau frappé has an inset rim at rest — the die's edge — and today's buttons have none. It is the one change visible without touching anything, so it is the one to look at hardest on the cream panel, where contrast is lowest. 2. The photograph takes the deboss. An inset shadow on .mv-tile cannot reach the image, because an inset paints below the element's children; it is applied to .mv-tile__img instead, where the photo is the background. Worth checking on a dark photo, where a 34% black inset has little left to darken. 3. Reduced motion needs animation: none, not just transform: none — every recoil here is a keyframe, and a blanket transform override leaves them running underneath it. Relief, shadow and the text flip all stay: none of those is motion.
