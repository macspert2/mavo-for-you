Tuiles — et si elles se comportaient comme des boutons ?

The site's tiles currently lift 2px on hover. This page puts the button's stamp — tilt, press, dashed perforation — on every real tile variant, at three strengths, and shows where it stops working. The geometry mirrors mv-tiles.css exactly; only the interaction differs.

Le rayon, à côté d'un vrai bouton

The buttons on the site settled at 4px. A tile is twenty times wider, and the same radius reads differently at that size — softly rounded on a 120px button, nearly square on a 320px card. The control above changes only the tiles; the button below stays at 4px, so the comparison is honest. My own preference is 6px: close enough to read as the same family, generous enough that a large card does not look like a table cell.

L'angle selon la largeur

A corner travels (width ÷ 2) × sin(angle). On a 320px card that is 3.4px at 1.2°; on a full-width row it is nearer 8px, and it stops reading as a stamp.

Ce que je retiendrais

Overlay and compact tiles take the stamp best — little or no body text, small boxes, and the perforation reads well over an image. Media cards with an excerpt want 0.6°: the tilt still registers, and a paragraph of rotated text does not. Result rows should never rotate, only press. The utility tile should do nothing at all — it is a container, and hover motion would promise a click that does not exist. Which is the real finding here: "tiles behave like buttons" is right for the tiles that are buttons, and wrong for the one that is not.

On the corner: 6px. 4px is literal consistency with the buttons and reads a touch severe once a card is 320px wide; 14px — today's value — now looks like a leftover from before the buttons became tickets. 6px keeps the family resemblance without pretending a card is a button. The compact variant drops to 4px alongside it, as it already runs tighter than the others in the theme.
